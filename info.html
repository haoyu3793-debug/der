About the park · Dublin · Ireland

Inside one of Europe's largest enclosed urban parks.

A 707-hectare green wedge of Dublin, walled-in since 1662, home to a fallow deer herd that has roamed unbroken for over three and a half centuries.

A 360-year story

From royal hunting ground to public park

"Phoenix" comes from the Irish Fionn Uisce — "clear water" — not the mythical bird

Phoenix Park was enclosed by James Butler, the 1st Duke of Ormonde, in 1662 as a royal hunting deer park for King Charles II. The original boundary wall — much of it still standing — encloses 707 hectares (1,750 acres), making it roughly twice the size of New York's Central Park and one of the largest walled urban parks in Europe.

The fallow deer that roam here today are direct descendants of the herd introduced for the Duke's hunt. The park was opened to the public in 1747, and its straight central spine, Chesterfield Avenue, was laid out the same year by Lord Chesterfield — then Lord Lieutenant of Ireland — who also commissioned the Phoenix Monument.

Today the park is managed by Ireland's Office of Public Works (OPW) and remains free to enter, 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

Things to see

Beyond the deer — eight landmarks worth a detour

Monument · 1861

Wellington Testimonial

62 m granite obelisk commemorating the Duke of Wellington — the tallest obelisk in Europe. Construction began in 1817 and took 44 years.

State residence · 1751

Áras an Uachtaráin

Built as the Vice-Regal Lodge in 1751. Official residence of the President of Ireland since 1938. Free Saturday tours.

Monument · 1747

Phoenix Monument

The Corinthian column erected by Lord Chesterfield that gives the park its name (despite the etymology being unrelated).

Monument · 1979

Papal Cross

35 m steel cross marking the spot where Pope John Paul II said Mass on 29 September 1979 — attended by ~1.25 million people.

Fort · 1735

Magazine Fort

Star-shaped fort on St Thomas's Hill, originally built to store gunpowder. Famously raided by the IRA in 1916 and 1939.

Zoo · 1831

Dublin Zoo

One of the world's oldest zoos, founded in 1831. 28 hectares inside the park, ticketed separately.

Castle · 16th c.

Ashtown Castle

Restored medieval tower house, now home to the OPW Visitor Centre and walled garden café.

Recreation

Fifteen Acres & Polo Grounds

Vast central grasslands hosting GAA, cricket, and the All Ireland Polo Club (founded 1872 — the oldest in Europe).

Watching the deer

How to watch the herd responsibly — our summary of the park’s published guidance

1

Stay 50 m back

Never approach. If a deer changes behaviour, you're too close.

2

Don't feed them

Human food causes serious illness and habituates deer to people. It is an offence.

3

No flash photography

Especially at dawn and dusk, when deer are most active. Use natural light.

4

Dogs on a lead at all times

Even calm dogs can trigger flight, injuries, or fawn abandonment.

5

No running near the herd

Joggers and cyclists should detour around groups, not through them.

6

Keep voices low

Sound carries on the open grasslands.

7

Drive slowly on Chesterfield Avenue

Speed limit 30 km/h across the whole park (lowered from 50 in February 2022). Deer cross unpredictably, especially at dusk.

8

Extra care during rut & fawning

Stags are aggressive in October. Does hide newborns alone in long grass during June — never touch a fawn.

⚠️

If you find an injured deer or a "lost" fawn

Don't intervene. Fawns are deliberately left alone by their mothers — they're not abandoned. If an animal is genuinely injured, call the Phoenix Park rangers on +353 87 228 9698 — they patrol 6.30am to 11.30pm, seven days a week. On a weekday you can also reach the Park Superintendent's office on +353 1 821 3021 (8am–5pm, Mon–Fri).

Numbers published by the OPW on phoenixpark.ie. We are not connected to the park service — if these have changed, check phoenixpark.ie/contact-us.

About the herd

One species, four colour morphs, one continuous lineage

Fallow deer (Dama dama)

Phoenix Park is home to only fallow deer — there are no red deer, sika, or muntjac in the park. The current population of around 600 individuals is descended from the herd introduced in the 1660s for the Duke of Ormonde's royal hunt.

  • Stags reach ~95 kg, does ~45 kg
  • Lifespan: 12–16 years in the wild
  • Rut (mating season): early to late October
  • Fawning: June; usually one fawn per doe
  • Population managed annually by OPW veterinarians

The four coat colours

Fallow deer show unusual variation in coat colour. All four morphs occur in the Phoenix Park herd, though some are far rarer than others.

Menil pale, spotted

Frequently asked

Questions we get asked

When is the best time to see the deer?

Just after sunrise and the hour before sunset, year-round. Midday in summer is the worst — deer retreat to woodland shade. The October rut is the most spectacular but also the most dangerous; stay well back.

Where am I most likely to see them?

Fifteen Acres (the central grassland) is the single most reliable spot. The grasslands bordering Áras an Uachtaráin and the area around Papal Cross are also very productive. Furry Glen is good for catching them in cover.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, but it must be on a short lead at all times. Dogs that chase or harass deer cause stress, injury, and fawn loss — and owners face significant fines.

Is the park safe at night?

The park is open 24 hours but vehicle gates close in the evening. Pedestrian gates remain accessible. Lighting is limited away from Chesterfield Avenue, so bring a torch and stick to main paths.

Are the deer ever dangerous?

They are wild animals. During the October rut, stags can charge humans they perceive as threats — every year there are incidents. In June, does will defend their fawns. Keep 50 m back and you'll be fine.

Why does the park have fallow deer specifically?

Fallow deer were the favoured species for European deer parks because they tolerate enclosure better than red deer and produce handsome trophy antlers. The Duke of Ormonde stocked the park with them in the 1660s and the population has been maintained ever since.
